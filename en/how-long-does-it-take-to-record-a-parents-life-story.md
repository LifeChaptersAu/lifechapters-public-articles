# How Long Does It Take To Record A Parent's Life Story?

![A LifeChapters family story book being shared with family.](<https://lifechapters.com.au/blog-assets/hero/41b338ce26d3edad4d02.delivery-v1.webp?hero=9b4a01e551b16466>)

Recording typically involves 3–5 sessions of 60–90 minutes each, spread over 2–4 weeks to respect your parent's energy. The post-production phase, including editing and audiobook narration, takes 4–8 weeks. This timeline allows for a polished result without requiring the family to manage the technical workload.

You are standing in the kitchen, the kettle just starting to boil, looking at the calendar and wondering if you have enough time left to do this properly. The idea of recording a parent's life story can feel like a massive project, and the uncertainty about how long it takes often stops people before they even start.

You are balancing work, family, and your parent's health, and adding a multi-week project to that list feels heavy. The timeline is actually much more manageable than it looks.

This post breaks down the real hours involved, separating your time from the technical work. You will see that the commitment is smaller than you might fear, and that the structure is designed to fit around your parent's energy, not the other way around.

## How many hours do I need to spend recording my parent's story?

The direct answer is that you will spend roughly 3 to 5 hours with your parent in total. This is broken into 3–5 sessions, each lasting 60–90 minutes. That is the entire amount of time you need to be present and engaged.

It is not a marathon, but rather a series of conversations. Think of it like a long lunch or an afternoon chat that happens a few times over a month. The goal is to capture the story in a way that feels natural, not to extract every detail in one go.

If you are worried about the logistics of sitting down for long periods, remember that these sessions are designed to be comfortable. You can do them at home, at a local café, or even via video call if you are interstate. The key is that the time is finite and clearly defined.

## What is the realistic timeline for editing and producing the final book?

This is where the timeline extends, but it is also where your work stops. The post-production phase, which includes editing, voice recreation, and audiobook narration, typically takes 4–8 weeks.

During this time, you do not need to be involved. You are not expected to edit audio, fix transcripts, or manage technical files. The professional service handles the heavy lifting. Your job is to wait, and perhaps to review a draft if you wish, but the active work is done by the team.

This separation is important. It means you can start the recording process without feeling like you are signing up for a months-long DIY project. You give them the raw material, and they return a finished piece.

## How does the recording schedule work around my parent’s energy levels?

Scheduling is about respecting your parent's daily rhythm. Spacing sessions 1–2 weeks apart allows for reflection and prevents fatigue. This pacing is crucial because a tired parent gives shorter, flatter answers.

The flexibility here is a major advantage. You are not bound by a studio schedule, but rather by your parent's comfort. If a session needs to be shorter, it can be. If they need a break, you take it. The timeline adapts to the person, not the other way around.

## What happens during the post-production phase that I don’t have to do?

The 4–8 week window is used for editing the transcript, recreating your parent's voice, and producing the audiobook. This is a complex technical process that requires specialised tools and time.

If you tried to do this yourself, the editing and production would require a significant amount of time. That is time spent cleaning up audio, writing chapters, and figuring out how to make the voice sound right. It is a significant burden, especially if you are already managing care or work.

With a managed service, that burden is removed. You do not need to learn audio software or understand voice synthesis. You just need to have the conversations. The rest is handled by people who do this every day.

## Can I record the story in one go or does it need to be spread out?

It needs to be spread out. Trying to record a life story in one sitting is a recipe for burnout, both for you and for your parent. A life is not a single event; it is a series of moments, and it takes time to uncover them.

Spreading the sessions over 2–4 weeks gives your parent time to settle into the idea. It also allows for a natural flow of memory. Often, a story mentioned in one session will trigger another in the next, and that connection is lost if you rush.

The spread-out approach also protects your relationship. You are not interviewing them; you are talking to them. The time between sessions allows the conversation to remain casual and warm, rather than feeling like a task to be completed.

## What people get wrong

**Thinking it takes months of their own time.** Many people assume they need to be involved in every step. In reality, your active time is just the 3–5 recording sessions. The rest is handled by the service.

**Trying to do it all themselves.** DIY recording can be done, but it comes with a significant editing burden. For most people, especially those with busy lives, this is a dealbreaker.

**Waiting for a specific time.** Some people prefer to wait for a holiday or a quiet period to start. While this is a practical scheduling preference, health changes and energy levels fluctuate. Starting with a few sessions now allows you to capture the story while it is fresh, rather than deferring it indefinitely.

## How do I fit this project into a busy work and family schedule?

The timeline is modular. You can fit the recording sessions into your existing life. A 60–90 minute session is similar to a long lunch or a family dinner.

You do not need to take time off work. You do not need to rearrange your entire calendar. You just need to find a few slots over a few weeks. The post-production happens in the background, so it does not compete for your attention.

This makes it accessible for people who are juggling careers, children, and care. The timeline is designed to be low-effort for you, high-impact for the family.

## What people get wrong about the time commitment

**Assuming the recording is the hard part.** The recording is actually the easy part; it is just talking. The hard part is the editing, which is why a professional service is so valuable.

**Thinking they need to be an editor.** You do not need to know how to edit audio. You just need to know how to listen; the technical skills are not required of you.

**Waiting until the last minute.** The 4–8 week post-production time is a minimum, and starting late might lead to disappointment with the quality. Starting earlier gives the team more time to do their best work.

**Ignoring the emotional side.** The timeline is not just about hours; it is about emotional energy, and spacing out the sessions helps protect that energy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I record my parent's story while they are in aged care?

Yes, sessions can be scheduled at the care facility. The professional handles the technical setup to minimize disruption to the resident's routine, so the time is just a conversation. This makes it feasible even when your parent is in a structured environment.

### What if my parent gets tired during the recording session?

Sessions are designed to be 60–90 minutes, with breaks encouraged. The professional will pause if needed, and the recording can be split across multiple days. The timeline is flexible enough to accommodate your parent's energy levels without rushing.

### How long does it take to record a parent's life story in total?

From start to finish, including recording sessions and post-production, the process typically takes 6–12 weeks. The recording sessions themselves take 3–5 hours of your time, spread over 2–4 weeks, while the professional handles the editing and production in the background.

### How do I know if my parent is ready to start recording?

There is no single sign that they are ready, but you can look for moments when they are relaxed and open to conversation. If they are willing to talk about a specific memory or share a story without prompting, that is a good time to gently suggest recording it. Respect their pace and be prepared to pause if they seem hesitant.

### What should I do if my parent refuses to record their life story?

You have tried to start this conversation, and it has hit a wall. You suggest recording their memories, and they give you a polite, firm no. It can feel like a rejection of you, or perhaps of the idea that their life's interesting enough to keep. In this case, respect their decision and do not push. You can try again later, or simply enjoy the conversations without recording them.
