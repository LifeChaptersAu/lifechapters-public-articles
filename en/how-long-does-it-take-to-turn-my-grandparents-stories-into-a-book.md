# How Long Does It Take To Turn My Grandparent's Stories Into A Book?

![A LifeChapters family story book being shared with family.](<https://lifechapters.com.au/blog-assets/hero/bd87bf2e8d75554c6364.delivery-v1.webp?hero=48f000f9303bbfb4>)

A professional-grade legacy book typically takes three to six months. This timeline covers structured interviewing, narrative editing, and production. It is significantly faster than the twelve to eighteen months often required for DIY transcription and writing, allowing you to plan around your grandparent’s health and availability without the burden of technical editing.

A common concern is that the window for capturing a life story is already closing. The project often feels like a massive undertaking, requiring months of free time or a level of technical skill that is difficult to acquire. There is a risk that by the time recordings are transcribed and the text edited, the person being preserved [has lost the](<https://lifechapters.com.au/blog/can-i-record-my-mothers-voice-if-she-has-early-stage-dementia>) energy to tell the story at all.

This is a frequent feeling, especially for Australian families balancing work and caring responsibilities. The gap between wanting to preserve a memory and having the capacity to execute a DIY project is often too wide to bridge alone. The timeline for a professional service is significantly shorter than a DIY approach, and it is designed to work around the reality of an aging grandparent rather than against it.

## How long does it actually take to turn recordings into a book?

For a professional legacy book, the standard timeline is three to six months. This covers the entire process from the first interview to the final production of the book and audiobook. This is significantly faster than the twelve to eighteen months often required for a do-it-yourself approach, which includes the heavy lifting of transcription, narrative structuring, and editing.

A fixed schedule provides a logistical benefit for health reasons. A shorter, structured timeline reduces the likelihood of losing the window for conversation due to health changes or simple fatigue. It allows the story to be captured while the grandparent is still able to engage with it, without the project dragging on for a year or more.

## What are the specific phases of the legacy book process?

The process breaks down into three clear phases, which helps demystify the timeline. First is discovery and interviewing, which typically takes two to four weeks. This is where the core stories are captured through structured conversations. Second is narrative structure and editing, which takes four to eight weeks. This is where the raw audio is transformed into a cohesive, readable narrative. Finally, design and production takes four to six weeks, resulting in the physical book and the audiobook.

## Why does DIY transcription take so much longer than professional editing?

The primary bottleneck in a DIY project is transcription. If an hour of conversation is recorded, it might take two to three hours just to transcribe it. Multiply that by ten or twenty hours of interviews, and the result is dozens of hours of tedious, error-prone work. On top of that, the transcript must be edited into a readable narrative, a skill that most people do not have and that takes a long time to develop.

Professional transcription removes the technical delay. The service handles the transcription and the narrative structuring, allowing the process to move forward steadily, week by week, rather than stalling under the weight of technical tasks.

## How does my grandparent’s health affect the timeline?

This is the most critical factor. A rigid DIY timeline often fails because it assumes a consistent level of energy and memory, which is not how aging works. A professional service is designed to be flexible. If the grandparent is tired one week, the interviews can pause. If they are in a good mood another week, more can be captured.

The timeline is built to accommodate this variability. The editing and production phases happen regardless of the interview pace, so even if the interviews take a little longer than expected, the final delivery date can still be met. This flexibility is essential for respecting the person’s needs while still achieving the goal of a finished book.

## What is the difference between a journal and a professional legacy book?

A journal is a prompt book that the grandparent fills out themselves. This approach places the entire burden of writing on the individual. Many grandparents find the blank pages intimidating, or they simply do not have the time or energy to write.

A professional legacy book takes the interviews and turns them into a polished narrative. It is a finished product, not a work in progress. It includes a physical book and an audiobook narrated in a recreated voice. It is a gift that requires no further effort from the grandparent once the interviews are done.

## How do I plan the timeline around my grandparent’s availability?

Start by looking at the calendar for the next three to six months. Identify the weeks where availability exists for conversations, keeping in mind that one hour is usually plenty. It is not necessary to be available every day; consistency is more important than intensity.

To start these conversations without feeling like an interrogation, focus on [open-ended questions about specific memories](<https://lifechapters.com.au/blog/how-to-get-a-parent-to-talk-about-their-life>) rather than broad life summaries. Asking about a particular day, place, or object often triggers detailed recollections more naturally than asking for a general history. This approach keeps the flow conversational and reduces the pressure on the speaker.

## What people get wrong

**Assuming it is a one-off event.** Many people think they can capture everything in one long afternoon. This rarely works. The energy is too high, and the depth is too shallow. Breaking it into smaller, regular sessions over a few weeks yields a much richer story.

**Waiting for the "perfect" time.** The perfect time is not a specific date; it is a period of stability. If waiting for a holiday or a birthday, the process is delayed. The interviews can happen any time, and the production will follow.

**Underestimating the emotional weight.** Capturing a life story is a significant emotional act. It is not just data entry. Giving time to process the memories as they come up is part of the timeline, not a distraction from it.

**Thinking it is just a gift.** It is a legacy, and the distinction matters because it changes how the conversation is approached. The focus shifts from the occasion to the content.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I speed up the process if my grandparent is in aged care?

Yes, but it requires coordination with care staff. The timeline may extend slightly due to scheduling constraints, but the professional editing and production phases remain the same duration. The key is to align interview times with the resident's routine to ensure they are rested and willing to talk.

### What if my grandparent doesn't want to be interviewed?

You can still create a book using existing photos, letters, and anecdotes shared by other family members. The timeline may be shorter as it relies on existing materials rather than new interviews. This approach is often more comfortable for grandparents who feel self-conscious about speaking directly to a recorder.

### Should I start the process before a specific milestone?

Yes, starting three to six months before a milestone like a birthday or anniversary is ideal. This buffer allows for the full interview and editing process without rushing, ensuring the story is captured while your grandparent is still able to engage comfortably with the conversations.

### Do I need to transcribe the recordings myself?

No, professional services handle transcription and narrative structure; you only review the final draft. This removes the most time-consuming and technical part of the process, allowing you to focus on the emotional content of the conversations rather than the mechanics of writing.

### What if my grandparent's memory is fading?

The process is designed to capture what is there now. Professionals use gentle prompting to elicit stories without pressure, and the timeline is flexible to accommodate their pace. It is about preserving the essence of their experience, not creating a perfect historical record, which makes it suitable for those with varying levels of cognitive clarity.
